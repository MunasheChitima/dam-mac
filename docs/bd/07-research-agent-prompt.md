# Long-running research-agent prompt

Use this prompt with a deep research agent:

```text
Act as a business development and product-strategy research agent for dam-mac.

You are doing a serious target-account study, not a lightweight brainstorm.
Work like a long-running research analyst. Use live web research, official docs,
API references, help centers, app marketplaces, release notes, product pages,
developer docs, pricing pages, and enterprise pages wherever possible.

Product context
- dam-mac is a digital asset management product built around a custom visual indexing engine.
- Its core value is helping people find, reuse, and create from the image and content libraries they already have, instead of generating new material by default.
- It is MCP-native, so the capability can pair with AI-assisted workflows.
- The current productized surface is on Mac, but do NOT treat it as Mac-only.
- The core engine was trained on Linux and can be hosted on a server.
- Think of this as an engine/capability that could be licensed, embedded, exposed as a plugin, or offered as OEM functionality.
- This is NOT a document-memory or generic file-search product.
- Do NOT confuse dam-mac with FindandSeek or any other document/agent-memory product.

Business question
We want to know who we should actually sell this to if we pursue a license/plugin/embedded-capability strategy.

Research objective
Find and evaluate companies that could realistically:
- license the capability
- embed it as a feature
- offer it as a plugin/integration
- OEM it inside an existing asset, brand, stock, or workflow platform
- partner strategically around visual-library intelligence

Prioritization logic
Prioritize companies that already own one or more of:
- visual asset libraries
- brand systems
- DAM or MAM products
- review/approval or creative workflow platforms
- stock media libraries
- AI-assisted content workflows
- developer-facing media platforms with APIs and extensibility

Deprioritize:
- pure video editors unless still strategically important
- generic AI startups with no content workflow
- companies that are mostly direct competitors unless there is still a credible BD path

Target categories to explore
- Canva / Pexels
- DAM platforms
- Brand management platforms
- Media asset management platforms
- Stock media marketplaces
- Creative workflow / review / approval platforms
- Developer-first media infrastructure platforms
- Enterprise content platforms with strong visual asset workflows

Seed companies
Start with, but do not limit yourself to:
- Canva
- Pexels
- Bynder
- Frontify
- Brandfolder
- Canto
- MediaValet
- Air
- Iconik
- Frame.io
- Cloudinary
- Adobe Experience Manager Assets
- Shutterstock
- Adobe Stock
- Getty Images / iStock
- Aprimo
- CELUM
- Image Relay
- Acquia DAM / Widen lineage if relevant

For each company, determine:
1. What the company actually does
2. Which category it belongs to
3. Why it is relevant to dam-mac
4. Whether it is a strong, medium, or weak fit
5. Whether it is primarily:
   - buyer
   - partner
   - competitor
   - mixed
6. What relevant features they already have today, especially around:
   - visual search
   - AI search
   - metadata enrichment
   - asset reuse
   - DAM/MAM
   - stock/media management
   - review/approval workflows
   - MCP, API, SDK, plugin marketplace, or app ecosystem
7. Where dam-mac might still add value despite those existing features
8. What the best commercial angle is:
   - license
   - embedded feature
   - plugin/integration
   - OEM
   - strategic partnership
   - corp dev / acquisition conversation
9. What team or buyer inside the company should see this:
   - product
   - AI/search
   - partnerships
   - corp dev
   - enterprise/platform
10. What the risk is:
   - too much feature overlap
   - already building internally
   - too much direct competition
   - weak extensibility
   - hard to access
   - unclear strategic fit

Important analytical distinctions
Be careful to distinguish between:
- a true DAM/MAM platform
- a stock marketplace
- a review/workflow platform
- an editor with a search feature
- a developer-facing media platform

Do not over-credit a company just because it has one AI search feature.
Call out the difference between:
- “has a narrow feature”
- “owns the broader workflow where dam-mac fits”

Do not just list companies.
Evaluate whether they are realistic targets worth founder time.

Deliverables
1. A ranked top-15 list of the best companies to approach
2. A top-5 shortlist of who to approach first
3. A table with these columns:
   - Company
   - Category
   - Current relevant features
   - Why dam-mac fits
   - Best commercial angle
   - Likely buyer/team
   - Competitive risk
   - Priority (1-5)
4. A section called “Companies to avoid first”
5. A section called “What proof points dam-mac still needs before outreach”
6. A section called “Most promising wedge by category”
7. A short final recommendation:
   - where to focus first
   - what to ignore
   - what messaging to use by target category

Research standards
- Prefer official sources.
- Use secondary commentary only to supplement.
- Be skeptical of vendor marketing.
- Note if a company already has strong native AI search, visual search, or DAM intelligence.
- Be honest if a company is more competitor than buyer.
- Optimize for a practical BD target list, not a generic market map.
```
