# Sell-to priority map for dam-mac

## Core principle

Do not start with video editors or NLE vendors.

Their search features are there to support editing. Your strongest value is not
"editing search." It is:

- making visual libraries easier to use
- helping teams create from what they already have
- increasing reuse of existing assets
- reducing unnecessary generation
- exposing that capability through MCP so AI-assisted workflows can call it

That means the best buyers are platforms that already own:

- a content library
- a brand or asset workflow
- a review and approval loop
- an app or API surface where retrieval can live as a capability

## Best-fit buyer categories

### Tier 1: strongest targets

These are the platforms I would put in front of first.

#### 1. Canva / Pexels

Why:

- owns the creation workflow
- owns a stock library through Pexels/Pixabay
- has an AI-generation surface
- retrieval-before-generation has a clear economic and product story
- MCP-native capability can pair with AI-assisted workflows

What you are selling:

- stronger use of owned and customer libraries
- less unnecessary generation
- a better visual-library entry point for creation

Buyer types:

- enterprise / brand asset product
- AI product / routing / Magic Studio
- corporate development / strategy

#### 2. DAM and brand-management platforms

Top names:

- Bynder
- Brandfolder
- Frontify
- Canto
- MediaValet
- Air

Why:

- their whole job is making asset libraries usable
- your value lands directly in search, enrichment, reuse, and AI-assisted access
- they already sell the business outcome you improve

What you are selling:

- better visual indexing
- stronger natural-language and visual retrieval
- MCP-native access to the library
- a differentiated AI/library story

Buyer types:

- product leadership
- AI/search platform leadership
- partnerships / corp dev

#### 3. Media asset management platforms

Top names:

- Iconik
- Adobe Frame.io, but only if the angle is media-library intelligence beyond review
- other cloud MAMs with archive/search positioning

Why:

- closer to long-term media organization than editors are
- they live and die on search, metadata, reuse, and workflow
- visual indexing can be sold as a capability, not a side feature

What you are selling:

- broader media-library intelligence
- MCP-native access for AI-assisted production workflows
- improved reuse across large archives

Buyer types:

- product
- search / metadata / AI
- partnerships

### Tier 2: selective targets

These are worth approaching only with a tighter angle.

#### 4. Stock and content marketplaces

Top names:

- Shutterstock
- Adobe Stock
- Getty / iStock
- Unsplash if there is a platform/integration route

Why:

- they own large content libraries
- better retrieval increases library value
- they may want better entry points into AI-assisted creation

Risk:

- many already have strong search and licensing stacks
- the pitch has to be about workflow value, not just better search

What you are selling:

- richer discovery
- higher utilization of owned inventory
- better integration into customer creation workflows

#### 5. Creative workflow and review platforms

Top names:

- Frame.io
- Approval Studio
- ReviewStudio
- PageProof

Why:

- they already sit inside the creative loop
- some already expose APIs or MCP surfaces
- they may want library intelligence as an extension of review and approval

Risk:

- review is not the same job as DAM
- this is best if they are expanding toward asset reuse and AI workflow

### Tier 3: weak initial targets

#### 6. NLE and editing vendors

Examples:

- Blackmagic / DaVinci Resolve
- Apple Final Cut Pro
- Adobe Premiere Pro

Why they are weaker:

- they already have native search features
- they are building editor-centric AI internally
- your value risks looking like one feature among many
- they are harder BD motions and less likely to buy externally at the start

Use them as:

- validation that the category is real
- later-stage strategic conversations

Not as:

- first outbound targets

## Who I would actually put this in front of

If I had to prioritize a concrete list, I would start here:

1. Canva
2. Pexels through Canva
3. Bynder
4. Frontify
5. Brandfolder
6. Iconik
7. Air
8. Canto
9. MediaValet
10. Frame.io

## Best commercial forms by buyer type

### Canva / Pexels

- strategic product conversation
- license
- embedded capability
- acquisition or acqui-hire only if real pull develops

### DAM / brand platforms

- licensing
- OEM capability
- AI-search / MCP feature module
- co-branded or "powered by" integration

### MAM / workflow platforms

- API or MCP-powered feature
- premium search / archive intelligence module
- integration into review / archive workflows

## What to say in one line

Use this:

> We built a visual DAM with a custom indexing engine that helps teams create
> from the assets they already have. We are looking for platforms where that
> capability can live as a licensed or embedded feature, rather than building a
> full standalone competitor to every workflow ourselves.

## Bottom line

If the question is "who should I actually sell this to?", the best answer is:

- not editors first
- not generic AI buyers
- **platforms that already own asset libraries, brand systems, or creative
  workflows and need stronger retrieval, reuse, and AI-assisted access**

That means your best first motions are:

1. Canva / Pexels
2. DAM platforms
3. MAM / creative workflow platforms
